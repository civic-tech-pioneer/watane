## Goal

Settles adversary RISK 1 and 2 on S158 (foreman's decision, recorded in the report). SQLite opens
`channels.db` by path (log TECHNICAL 4.8), so whoever can write a parent of the log directory
could swap the parent after the anchor is taken and have SQLite open a database they own: the own
head would then come from it (a second entry at a published seq forks the own channel) and
`drop_identity`'s `IdentityInUse` check could be bypassed. After this task the store open
**detects** such a swap: after `Connection::open`, the file SQLite opened is the same inode as the
`channels.db` on the checked anchor, or the open fails with an error at the path (no other state
changes). What still cannot be closed without a VFS (a swap and swap-back inside the window, and
the `-wal`/`-shm` names) is written into TECHNICAL 7 "Not protected", with its consequences.
And log TECHNICAL 4.7's sentence that "a swapped parent redirects nothing" is made accurate:
before the first check, a swap can point the path at another directory of the participant's own
with mode 0700 (pre-existing; nothing is readable to the attacker); say that.

1. AC1 Unix: after `Connection::open`, compare `(st_dev, st_ino)` of `statat(anchor,
   "channels.db", AT_SYMLINK_NOFOLLOW)` with the database file SQLite has open. Prefer SQLite's
   own handle (e.g. `PRAGMA`/`sqlite3_file_control(SQLITE_FCNTL_FILE_POINTER)` via rusqlite's
   `handle()`, or any documented way to reach its fd); if none is reachable safely, fall back to
   `stat` of the path right after the open and say in TECHNICAL that this narrows, not closes,

## Fetch: HumanLayer RPI to CRISPY (ZenML)

https://www.zenml.io/llmops-database/evolving-ai-coding-agent-workflows-from-research-plan-implement-to-crispy

Prompt: Summarize the failure modes of RPI found (plan document length, human review, alignment) and what CRISPY changed (design discussion, question phases, reading code vs plans, instruction budgets). Include specific numbers.

**Failure modes of the original RPI approach**

- **Plan length:** Plans ran about 1,000 lines for features that produced about 1,000 lines of code. Reviewing a plan took roughly an hour and was nearly as much work as reviewing the code, so it gave little leverage.
- **Human review:** Engineers often didn't read the generated code. Implementations frequently diverged from the plans, so reviewers had to read both, and engineers became attached to plans that no longer matched the code. HumanLayer's advice to skip reading code was reversed after six months, and they had to replace large parts of systems built that way.
- **Alignment:** About 50% of the time, agents skipped the interactive steps and wrote full plans without asking questions. Users had to include "magic words" to get back-and-forth behavior.
- **Other issues:** The 85+ instruction planning prompt exceeded the roughly 150-200 instructions frontier models can reliably follow. Passing raw tickets to research produced opinions instead of facts, and plans tended to be horizontal (all database changes, then all services, then all APIs), which made incremental verification hard.

**What CRISPY changed**

- **Seven stages:** Context, Research, Iterate (design discussion), Structure, Plan, sYnthesize, and Implement.
- **Question phase:** A separate context window turns the ticket into research questions, and research runs without the ticket so it gathers facts rather than implementation opinions.
- **Design discussion:** A roughly 200-line document covering current state, desired end state, patterns, decisions, and open questions. HumanLayer claims it offers about 5x the leverage of reviewing a 1,000-line plan.
- **Structure outline:** About two pages, versus about eight for a full plan. It lays out vertical slices with test checkpoints, not horizontal layers.
- **Plan:** Because the design and structure are already aligned, engineers spot-check the plan and focus their detailed review on the code.
- **Reading code:** Engineers are expected to read and own the actual code rather than rely on plans.
- **Instruction budgets:** Prompts were decomposed from the 85+ instruction monolith to under 40 instructions each. Context is kept under about 40% use (the "dumb zone" begins around 80,000 of 168,000 usable tokens), with workflow wrapping considered at 60%.
- **Goal:** A more reliable 2-3x productivity gain without the "slop" that would need rework, with the presenter describing 2026 as "the year of no more slop."
